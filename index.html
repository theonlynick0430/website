You can just learn things.

Future models may solve hard problems with little human help, which raises the question of what is still worth learning. My answer: it depends on how much you care. If a question matters to you, the how and why are worth understanding, even if AI gets to the answer first.

For the past six months I've used AI as a personal tutor to learn subjects I'd never studied: generating visuals, digitizing handwritten notes, working through confusing concepts, and running experiments. Below are my notes and references, plus examples of how I've used these tools, in case they help someone else figure out where to start. (Original thread.)

  1. Annotated reference · Nov 2026

    The Principles of Diffusion Models, annotated

    My adapted version of Lai, Song, Kim, Mitsufuji & Ermon (2025). My notes are written directly into the text: skipped steps expanded inside the derivations, added proofs (e.g. divergence theorem, multivariate integration by parts, Jensen's inequality), and highlights on the parts I found most important.
